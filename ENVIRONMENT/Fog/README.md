# Player-centred visibility fog

PlayerDistanceFog owns one full-screen depth overlay for the active top-down presentation. It reconstructs opaque surface world positions and fades them by horizontal distance from the player, with broad stationary edge variation. Terrain, opaque zombies and props progressively disappear into grey-blue fog. Perspective and orthographic views use the same inverse camera matrices; this is a world-space radius, not a screen vignette.

Defaults in ENVIRONMENT/Configuration/GameplayEnvironmentMaster.tres: enabled, clear to 24m, full fog at 48m, opacity 1, edge variation 2m, color (0.39,0.46,0.48). Full radius is clamped to at least clear radius + 1m. Top-down tuning panel exposes enabled, both radii, opacity, variation and tint; Copy settings includes all six values (color as RGBA array). Fixed tint is independently adjustable, including for night; it does not automatically inherit Sky3D lighting.

WorldController creates the owner under SystemsRoot/EnvironmentRoot; its camera-local DistanceFogOverlay is intentionally a camera child for correct fullscreen culling and lifetime. It has no physics, casts no shadows and draws late in the transparent pass (priority 127), blending directly over the scene without a second viewport or screen-color copy. Interface controls are unaffected. FPS overrides initialization and creates no visibility-fog owner/overlay; its existing sky/weather remain unchanged.

No C# process/physics callbacks. Player PositionChanged updates one uniform while moving; settings uploads happen only on initialization/UI edits. OFF or zero opacity hides the overlay (no draw) and removes the position subscription. Reactivation explicitly refreshes the center. Exit removes subscription and frees the camera overlay. Enabled fog costs one depth read and simple arithmetic per screen pixel; no enemy scans, rays, FogVolumes or volumetric simulation. Existing vegetation/weather/enemies keep their own activation logic.

This is visual atmosphere, not explored-map fog of war, enemy spawning, AI line-of-sight or simulation culling. Hidden zombies continue their existing behavior and become visible as they approach. Depth comes from opaque surfaces: transparent effects/glass can inherit fog distance from opaque geometry behind them, especially at silhouettes. A rectangular prefab or transparent fading effect is not independently tracked. Radius boundaries have soft variation, not animated wisps. Near-camera obstruction fading remains separate.

Source review: Godot advanced post-processing guidance for reverse-Z depth reconstruction/fullscreen culling. No global rendering/project settings changed.
